The Greens
Email Sequence

Three emails. Forty days. No pitch until there is a conversation. The brief does not leave the room until they are in it.

Operating Principles

What makes this work.

Olajuwon and Earl are post-Hulu, post-raise, and managing real inbound. The failure mode for most cold outreach at this level is pitching too fast. The concept arrives before trust does. They evaluate the idea, find it interesting, and never respond because they have no reason to — they got the concept for free and there is no relationship behind it.

P1
Email 1 contains no offer and no concept. An observation only. It earns a reply by making them feel accurately seen, not sold.
P2
Email 2 delivers value independent of whether they ever respond. A framework they can use regardless. This is what builds credibility and distinguishes the sequence from a follow-up chase.
P3
Email 3 makes the offer clear and brief. One ask. One time frame. No re-pitch. They either take the call or they don't.
P4
The Greens brief is never attached, linked, or described in any email. It is introduced as a built concept in a call. The purpose of the emails is to get to a call. Once the call is scheduled, the brief is how the call opens.

If they reply to any email before Email 3, move to the diagnostic offer immediately. Do not continue the planned sequence. Respond to what they give you.

Email 01 / Day 0

The observation.

Pure pattern recognition. No ask. No mention of Ecrof's work. No setup for what comes next. The only goal is a reply that says something like "what do you mean?" or "say more."

If They Reply

How to respond to any reply.

The sequence is a guide, not a script. If they engage at any point, abandon the sequence and respond to what they actually said. A few patterns:

R1
If they reply with curiosity ("say more" / "what do you mean?"): Do not send the next planned email. Send the diagnostic offer immediately. They opened the door. Walk through it.
R2
If they reply skeptically ("we've looked at this" / "not the right time"): Acknowledge it. Agree that the timing question is real. Ask one question: "When would the right time be?" Do not defend. Do not pitch. Listen.
R3
If they forward to a team member: That person is now the path. Respond to them directly. Same sequence principles apply — earn the relationship before the concept arrives.
R4
If there is no reply to any of the three emails: One final message, seven days after Email 3: "Should I close the loop on this?" That is the entire message. If no response after that, close the loop and move on.
